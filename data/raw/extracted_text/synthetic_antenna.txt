A Synthetic Multi-Band Antenna Design for Testing
Abstract - This paper presents a synthetic antenna design created for validation of automated figure
extraction pipelines. The antenna operates at 2.4 GHz and 5.8 GHz with satisfactory return loss. Results are
shown in Fig. 1 through Fig. 3.
I. INTRODUCTION Antenna design is a critical aspect of
modern wireless communication systems. The proposed
antenna in this work uses a rectangular patch on FR-4
substrate. The geometry of the antenna is shown in Figure 1.
The substrate has a dielectric constant of 4.4 and thickness
of 1.6mm. Optimizations were performed using HFSS to
achieve dual-band operation.
Fig. 1. Geometry of the proposed dual-band rectangular patch
antenna.
The antenna was fabricated and measured using a vector
network analyzer. The simulated and measured S11
parameters are compared in Fig. 2 and show good
agreement. The radiation patterns shown in Fig. 3 confirm
omnidirectional behavior in the H-plane.

III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION The measured return loss
at 2.4 GHz is -18.5 dB and at 5.8 GHz is -22.3 dB, which are
well below the -10 dB threshold. The bandwidth at each
resonance exceeds 100 MHz. The gain was measured to be
4.2 dBi at 2.4 GHz.
The current distribution at 2.4 GHz shows concentration
along the patch edges, confirming the fundamental TM10
mode excitation. Further parametric studies on the feed
position are discussed next.
Fig. 3. Measured radiation patterns at 2.4 GHz and 5.8 GHz.
IV. CONCLUSION A dual-band rectangular patch antenna
has been designed, fabricated, and tested. The antenna
operates at 2.4 GHz and 5.8 GHz with return loss better than
-15 dB at both bands as demonstrated in Fig. 2. The radiation
patterns in Figure 3 show stable omnidirectional coverage.
Future work will explore MIMO configurations.
