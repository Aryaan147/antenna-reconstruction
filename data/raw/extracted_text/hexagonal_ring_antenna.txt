Abstract—A compact stub loaded hexagonal ring shape 
antenna using CPW feed is designed and implemented 
for WLAN/WiMAX/ITU application. Y- Shape stub is 
loaded inside hexagonal ring to achieve multiband 
performance with miniaturization. The proposed 
antenna is highly compact with (20 × 20 × 0.8) mm³ 
dimension. The impedance bandwidth of the antenna is 3 
GHz for both the band respectively from 2.9 to 5.9 GHz 
and 7 to 10 GHz. The substrate material used is Flame 
Retardent-4 (FR-4) due to its availability and low cost of 
fabrication. Relative dielectric constant and substrate 
thickness of material is 4.4 and 0.8 mm respectively. 
Ansys HFSS tool is used to optimize the designed 
parameters and experimental validation is done by 
Vector Network Analyzer. 
Keywords—Y shape stub, CPW feed, multiband, 
hexagonal ring, miniaturization. 
I. INTRODUCTION 
 
Today’s communication technology demands wide 
bandwidth to integrate various applications in a single 
device. Multiple wireless standards must be supported for 
versatility of the device. Due to its conformal nature, 
microstrip antennas are preferred. The low cost, simple 
fabrication and easy integration in different circuitry has 
made microstrip antennas very favorable. Currently 
different techniques of multiband and wideband antenna for 
WLAN/ WiMAX/ ITU/ X-Band have been proposed [1-
4].To achieve better performance in antenna application 
different type of slots and strips such as inverted F and L 
shaped slots used to create dual and triple band 
performance. Different shape of strip added in radiating 
element to achieve triple band characteristics [5, 6]. Ring 
type radiator and stub loading have unique properties which 
help to achieve both compactness and multiband response 
simultaneously [7]. Meta-material structure has negative 
permittivity and negative permeability which helps to 
realize multiband performance [8, 9]. Split Ring Resonator 
is a basic structure of permittivity; hence the improved 
performance can be achieved without affecting the size of 
radiating element. The Split Ring Resonator (SRR) and 
Complementary Split Ring Resonator (CSRR) based dual-
band and triple-band antenna were discussed [10, 11]. 
Fractal geometry also provides antennas for dual-band and 
wide-band applications [12]. Although mentioned methods 
are proposed for creating dual-band [1], triple-band [2, 3] 
and multi-band [4] structures with compact size, wide-band 
nature with multi-band application integrability in a single 
compact antenna was seldom discussed. 
 
II. ANTENNA GEOMETRY 
The proposed stub loaded antenna uses FR-4 substrate with 
relative dielectric constant and loss tangent of 4.4 and 0.02 
respectively, with a 50 coplanar waveguide (CPW) 
transmission line feed. The thickness of the substrate is X = 
0.8 mm. It has a feed length (F L) of 7.5 mm and feed width 
FW of 1.2 mm. Antennas of different evolution stages are 
shown in Fig.1 from #1 to #3.

III. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION 
The working and parametric analysis is performed using 
High Frequency Structural Simulator (HFSS), which uses 
Finite Element Method (FEM) technique to validate the 
results.  
 
 
 
Fig.4: Comparative simulated S11 graphs of the antenna design steps 
 
 
It can be observed that each stage has constant overall 
dimensions (L  × W). Fig.3 depicts the radiation efficiency 
of the three different antennas. Antenna (#1) has resonant 
frequencies at 4.4 GHz and 6.6 GHz. A broad bandwidth of 
4.1 GHz (3.5 GHz – 7.6 GHz) is observed but lower 
frequencies are not covered.   To achieve resonance at the 
lower side, the ground plane is tapered in antenna (#1), 
hence antenna (#2) is obtained as shown in Fig.1b. Antenna 
(#2) shows resonance at 4.1 GHz covering a bandwidth of 
3.4 GHz (3 GHz – to 6.4Hz). Due to tapering of ground 
plane lower resonance is maintained but resonance at higher 
frequency is lost in antenna (#2). Tapering off the ground 
plane transforms the impedance linearly at the lower band. 
In square shaped ground plane resonance was obtained at 
3.5GHz. After tapering the resonance shifted to 3GHz due 
to increased electrical length, it is depicted in antenna (#2). 
In order to compensate the loss of resonance at higher

IV. CONCLUSION 
Compact stub loaded hexagonal ring antenna is designed 
and implemented for WLAN, WiMAX and ITU 
applications. The proposed antenna covers the entire band 
with broader bandwidth. Stable radiation pattern is achieved
with low cross polarization. Experimental results such as 
return loss and VSWR are in well agreement with the 
simulated results of the proposed antenna. Miniaturization 
with multiband performance is achieved using ring type 
structure with stub loading and it makes the prototype a 
better candidate for wireless application. Gain of the
prototype can be maximized using artificial magnetic 
conductor as ground plane.
