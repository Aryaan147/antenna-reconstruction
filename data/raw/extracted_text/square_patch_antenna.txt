Square patch antenna with circular polarization for 
WLAN application 
Shaik subhani1, Ravi Prakash Dwivedi2 
School of Electronics Engineering 
VIT University, Chennai, India 
Shaik.subhani2014@vit.ac.in1, raviprakash.dwivedi@vit.ac.in2 
 
Abstract— A compact square patch antenna is studied with 
different feeding techniques. The proposed antenna operates at 
band of 2.4 GHz for the application of WLAN. Simulation results 
evaluated in term of return loss, bandwidth, radiation pattern, 
directivity, gain and axial ratio. Gain of the proposed antennas is 
3.28 dB for trimmed square patch and 2.4 dB for inset feed 
antenna and 2.8 dB for centre feed antenna. 
 
Keywords— Square patch antenna, WLAN, circular polarization. 
I. I NTRODUCTION 
Development of modern wireless system requires precision 
system and antenna with better performance. Circular 
polarization antennas are one such a kind to their applications 
in satellite communications, Navigation systems, and mobile 
communications, WLAN, Wi-MAX, WPAN and RFID [1]. 
The advantages of circular polarization over other 
polarizations are immunity to multipath fading, resistant to 
Faraday rotation effect and antennas are not restricted in its 
orientation while transmission and reception. Design of 
antennas for wireless communication systems should have 
low cost, compact design and better performance such as wide 
bandwidth and high gain.  
We implemented antennas using transmission line model 
approach because of its advantages as high performance with 
less complexity [1]. It gives more information about 
geometric design of antenna. In conventional antenna design 
we have more freedom due to distributed nature of the antenna 
[9]. It is just like a RLC circuit we need to optimize according 
to the requirement so that it is easy of all and simple to design 
in this method of analysis [10]. Different feed techniques are 
followed so that we can evaluate a better model because each 
technique has its own advantage over other feed techniques 
like strip, probe etc. As it was designed in transmission line 
model we have advantages in impedance matching.  
The proposed antenna is designed to be operated 
in the 2.4 GHz (2.25 GHz – 2.57 GHz for wireless 
applications. The designed antennas are passive 
structure operates at its half wavelength mode [2]. 
With reference to RFIDs, Gain and Circular 
Polarization (CP) are very important parameters. 
The patch elements of most commonly used shapes such as 
square, rectangular and circular   are   radiating   primarily in 
linear polarization [9]. To modify polarization of the patch we 
use conventional feed or slight changes in the patch it results 
circular or elliptical polarization. In conventional feed method 
there are no modifications in the patch but other method 
require changes in the patch and might be in the feed 
arrangements. Recently multi band antenna has greatest 
advantage we can design multiband antennas using different 
methods of implementation like slot loading, Fractal, multi 
stacking and modified patches. Whatever the method we 
choose to design multiband antenna polarization of the bands 
must be same so that it can be used in real time. 
Gain is one of the defining factors in determining the 
reading range of the RFID while CP is required so that the 
signal can be captured effectively and successfully. Therefore, 
it is a need to have an antenna that has a high gain and the 
antenna possesses a CP instead of a linear.

IV. CONCLUSION 
The proposed antenna is valid for wireless communication 
systems and suitable candidate for other microwave 
applications. Proposed designs resonate at 2.4GHz frequency 
which has applications of WLAN. From comparison table.1 
we can conclude that for gain and compact size trimmed 
square gives better results 3.28 dB gain with size 45 × 45× 1.6 
mm3.  For the gain and bandwidth centre feed antenna gives 
better results 2.8 dB, 250 MHz respectively. Trimmed square 
patch and other antennas can be further enhanced using wide 
band technique and can be used with integrating active 
components.
